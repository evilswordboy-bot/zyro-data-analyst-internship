WEEK 5 
 
Advanced Business Intelligence & Decision Analytics 
Project: Ride Analytics & Revenue Intelligence Platform 
Project Progress 
Week 2: Ride demand, locations, customer behavior, peak periods, and new vs repeat customers were analyzed. 
Week 3: Revenue patterns and driver performance were analyzed using operational and financial KPIs. 
Week 4: These findings were transformed into an interactive Power BI dashboard with KPIs, visuals, and filters. 
Current Stage: Week 5 upgrades the dashboard into an advanced analytical and decision-support system. Week 6 will be 
the final end-to-end completion, validation, presentation, and evaluation. 
Goal: Explain not only what happened, but why the observed patterns matter and what the business should investigate 
next. 
1. Understand the Business Problem 
• Move beyond descriptive charts and analyze relationships between demand, revenue, customers, drivers, 
cancellations,ratings, distance, ride type, and location. 
• Every major conclusion must be supported by a measurable KPI, comparison, trend, or segment. 
2. Advanced Data Model 
• Review relationships, data types, duplicate IDs, missing keys, invalid dates, impossible distances, negative or zero 
fares,and inconsistent statuses. 
• Create or improve a dedicated Date table where applicable and document the main relationships. 
• Keep source data, calculated columns, measures, and presentation logic clearly separated. 
3. Advanced DAX Measures 
• Create Total Revenue, Completed Rides, Cancellation Rate, Completion Rate, Average Fare, Average Distance, 
AverageRating, and Revenue per Completed Ride. 
• Add previous-period values, absolute change, percentage change, ranking, and contribution-percentage measures 
wheresupported. 
• Validate important measures against SQL or Python calculations. 
4. Revenue & Segment Intelligence 
• Compare revenue by month, location, ride type, payment method, and driver. 
• Identify high-volume/low-revenue and lower-volume/high-average-fare segments. 
• Do not claim profitability unless cost data exists.

5. Customer Segmentation 
• Create meaningful customer segments using ride frequency or available behavior data. 
• Compare high-frequency and low-frequency customers, repeat-customer contribution, average fare, ride frequency, 
andpreferred ride types. 
• Document the evidence and business meaning of each segment. 
6. Driver Performance Intelligence 
• Build a driver view using rides, completed rides, cancellations, revenue, rating, and completion rate. 
• Create a clearly documented ranking metric and compare drivers against relevant averages. 
• Investigate unusual combinations such as high volume with high cancellation rate. 
7. Cancellation & Operational Analysis 
• Analyze cancellation rate by hour, weekday, location, ride type, and other available dimensions. 
• Identify where cancellations concentrate and whether they overlap with high-demand periods. 
• Clearly state that correlation does not prove causation. 
8. Advanced Power BI Features 
• Add a dedicated advanced-analysis page. 
• Use appropriate features such as drill-through, report tooltips, bookmarks, decomposition tree, key influencers, 
conditionalformatting, or dynamic titles where supported. 
• Advanced does not mean overcrowded. 
9. Scenario / What-If Analysis 
• Create at least one practical scenario if the dataset supports it, such as estimated revenue change from a 
hypotheticalchange in completed rides, average fare, or cancellation rate. 
• Clearly label scenario results as estimates and document all assumptions. 
10. Business Questions 
• Which periods or locations combine high demand with high cancellation rates? 
• Which segments generate the largest share of completed-ride revenue? 
• Which customer segments contribute most to rides or revenue? 
• Which drivers differ most from the relevant benchmark? 
• Which locations have strong demand but weaker revenue per completed ride? 
• Which areas deserve further investigation before the final project? 
11. Advanced Business Insights 
• Produce at least 8 evidence-based insights. 
• Include findings covering demand, revenue, customers, drivers, and cancellations. 
• Use actual values and percentages from the dataset wherever possible. 
12. Business Recommendations 
• Produce at least 5 practical recommendations linked directly to findings. 
• Recommendations may address peak-period supply, cancellations, retention, driver operations, pricing analysis, 
orlocation strategy. 
• Separate dataset-supported recommendations from ideas requiring additional data or testing.
